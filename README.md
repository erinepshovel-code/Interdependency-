The Interdependent Way

Author: Erin Spencer
Status: Canonical / Versioned
Context: Master’s Thesis (Indisciplinary)


---

Overview

The Interdependent Way is an indisciplinary ethical and psychological framework that defines interdependency as reciprocal competence, rather than mutual vulnerability or moral obligation.

The framework integrates:

depth psychology (explicitly Jungian),

competence-based ethics,

symbolic and archetypal cognition,

and resonance as a functional psychological mechanism.


This repository serves as the canonical specification of the framework.
All derivative interpretations are non-authoritative unless explicitly endorsed by the author.


---

Theoretical Foundation

This work is explicitly grounded in the analytical psychology of Carl Jung, particularly his treatment of archetypes as autonomous psychological operators rather than metaphors or cultural ornaments.

Within this framework:

Archetypes are treated as structurally real patterning agents in cognition and behavior.

Mythic and symbolic language is treated as a necessary interface layer between pre-verbal cognition and explicit reasoning.

Resonance is treated as a non-propositional coherence signal that governs symbolic selection, compression, and transmission within the psyche.


These elements are not decorative. They are methodological.


---

On Resonance

Resonance, as used here, does not function as a truth claim, metaphysical assertion, or aesthetic preference.

It is defined operationally as:

> The coherence signal produced when symbolic structures align with pre-linguistic cognitive architectures (e.g., archetypes, affective schemas, embodied memory), influencing attention, retention, and action prior to rational adjudication.



This definition is consistent with depth psychology, narrative cognition, and affective neuroscience, while remaining explicitly bounded.


---

Indisciplinary Position

This work is indisciplinary, not interdisciplinary.

It does not attempt to synthesize established disciplines at their boundaries.
Instead, it interrogates the pre-disciplinary symbolic and psychological substrates from which those disciplines emerge.

As such, resonance and archetypal structures are treated as foundational rather than optional.


---

Scope and Limits

This framework:

does not assert cosmological, theological, or supernatural claims,

does not rely on personal revelation or spiritual authority,

does not claim empirical falsifiability where such criteria are inappropriate.


It does:

formalize symbolic and mythic structures as psychological instruments,

constrain resonance to defined cognitive roles,

and provide explicit limits on interpretation and application.
